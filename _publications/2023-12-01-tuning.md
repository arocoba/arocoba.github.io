Semiconductor random lasers combine the compactness and electrical pumping of conventional laser diodes with optical feedback generated by disorder. In this work, we demonstrate a simple and reproducible method to tune the emission properties of electrically pumped semiconductor random lasers through controlled pulsed laser ablation of the output facet.

By introducing engineered surface disorder, the optical feedback experienced by the laser cavity can be modified, leading to significant changes in the spectral and temporal characteristics of the emitted light. The fabricated devices exhibit random-lasing behaviour, including multimode emission and complex spectral dynamics, while maintaining electrical operation and compatibility with standard semiconductor laser technology.

The study analyzes how the degree of disorder affects the laser emission, providing insight into the relationship between cavity morphology, scattering strength, and modal dynamics. The results show that laser ablation offers a versatile tool for tailoring the properties of semiconductor random lasers without requiring complex fabrication procedures.

These findings contribute to the development of electrically driven disordered photonic devices and provide a practical route towards compact random-laser sources with customizable optical characteristics.
